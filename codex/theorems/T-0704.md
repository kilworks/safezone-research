# T-0704

Extracted from [theory/07-zombies-and-pursuit.md](../../theory/07-zombies-and-pursuit.md), where the full context, the proof and the test are.

**Theorem [T-0704](../theorems/T-0704.md).** (a) Every zero-drain escape of [T-0702](../theorems/T-0702.md) can be run without breaking the speed rule. Every zero-drain threshold under B1 (at most 1.9 m/s for a regular zombie, 2 m/s for a still guard, 3.4 m/s for a chasing guard, all from contact) is below 8.94 m/s. The rule is not a hard limit: moving faster "counts as being outside" ([M-0105](../rules/zone.md#m-0105)), at a cost set by [U-0113](../unknowns.md#u-0113). At 8.94 m/s from contact, the time in range of a regular zombie is $5/7.54 = 250/377 \approx 0.663$ s and of a chasing guard $\approx 1.326$ s. Under B0 even the fastest legal escape pays the first tick.

(b) Under [U-0112](../unknowns.md#u-0112) reading A (the average is the path length over a sliding 60 s window, threshold 8.94 m/s), a contestant who never breaks the 1 min rule gains at most $60 \times 8.94 - 60 \times 1.4 = 452.4$ m on a pure-pursuit zombie in any 60 s window during which the zombie never reaches them ($d > 0$ throughout). With the exact threshold of reading B, $20$ mph $= 5588/625 = 8.9408$ m/s (international mile of 1609.344 m), the bound is $56556/125 = 452.448$ m. Straight flight at the threshold speed reaches the bound.

(c) The condition $d > 0$ in (b) cannot be dropped. A contestant who stands still in contact for 15 s (the zombie on them, distance 0) and then runs straight at $536.4/45 = 11.92$ m/s for 45 s respects both speed rules under reading A (60 s mean exactly 8.94, every 15 s mean at most 11.92 < 13.41) and gains $536.4 - 1.4 \times 45 = 473.4$ m in that window: the time spent at distance 0 costs the contestant nothing, because the zombie cannot close a gap that is already 0.

*Proof.* (a) Compare the numbers of [T-0702](../theorems/T-0702.md)(c) and (d) with 8.94; $L = 5/(8.94 - 1.4)$ and $10/(8.94 - 1.4)$. (b) Where $d > 0$, [T-0700](../theorems/T-0700.md)(b) gives $d' \le |E'| - u$; integrating over the window, $d(t + 60) - d(t) \le \int_t^{t+60} |E'| \, ds - 60u$, and the path length is at most $60 \times 8.94$ under reading A. Equality holds for straight flight at constant speed, where both move on one line. (c) During the first 15 s the distance stays 0 ([T-0700](../theorems/T-0700.md)(b), the contestant does not move); afterwards both move on one line and the distance grows at $11.92 - 1.4$ m/s for 45 s. $\square$

Under reading C of [U-0112](../unknowns.md#u-0112) (endpoint displacement instead of path length), a winding path can be longer than 536.4 m in one window, and [T-0700](../theorems/T-0700.md)(b) bounds the gain by path length, not displacement. No bound is proven here for that reading.

*Label:* (a) `CONDITIONAL` on [U-0425](../unknowns.md#u-0425) and [U-0429](../unknowns.md#u-0429) (through [T-0702](../theorems/T-0702.md)), with the 8.94 m/s value `DOCUMENTED` ([M-0105](../rules/zone.md#m-0105)); the cost of going faster is [U-0113](../unknowns.md#u-0113). (b) and (c) `CONDITIONAL` on [U-0112](../unknowns.md#u-0112) reading A (B for the exact value), and [U-0424](../unknowns.md#u-0424). *Test:* `test_t0704_speed_rule` (exact fractions; collinear equality case; random paths whose path length over the 60 s window is exactly $60 \times 8.94$ m, the 1 min rule only: the 15 s rule is not enforced there, and enforcing it would only remove paths, so the bound is unaffected; the counterexample of (c) in exact fractions).
