# T-0706

Extracted from [theory/07-zombies-and-pursuit.md](../../theory/07-zombies-and-pursuit.md), where the full context, the proof and the test are.

**Theorem [T-0706](../theorems/T-0706.md).** (a) Under [U-0424](../unknowns.md#u-0424) reading B and convention K, a zombie at distance 0 from its target keeps that target, and stays at distance 0, for as long as the target moves at 1.4 m/s or slower and the zombie exists.

(b) So, under reading B and K, a zombie summoned or raised by a horde bonus starts on its owner and stays on an owner who moves at 1.4 m/s or slower until the zombie disappears ([M-0450](../rules/zombies.md#m-0450)). Its lifetime is unknown ([U-0612](../unknowns.md#u-0612), swept from 60 s to 86400 s), so an escort may last as little as a minute. While it lasts, the owner, immune, carries the zombie with them: every other contestant within 5 m of the owner is within 5 m of the zombie and is drained. With $k$ such escorts, a visitor loses $k$ HP per 10 s, up to 30 HP per escort ([U-0008](../unknowns.md#u-0008)), and the owner receives it ([M-0635](../rules/zombies.md#m-0635), [U-0613](../unknowns.md#u-0613)) and earns one blood token per HP drained ([M-0630](../rules/economy.md#m-0630), measured as in [U-0613](../unknowns.md#u-0613) reading D). A death zombie that appears $r \le 10$ m from its owner and keeps the owner as its target until it reaches them (for example when no other contestant becomes strictly nearer to the zombie before then) reaches the owner within $r/(1.4 - v)$ s if the owner moves at speed at most $v < 1.4$ (at most 7.14 s for a still owner at $r = 10$), and then becomes an escort. Being the nearest contestant at the moment of appearance is not enough: with a still owner, a death zombie 10 m away and another contestant 11 m from the zombie on the far side, running straight at it at 3 m/s, the two gaps shrink at 1.4 and 1.6 m/s, are both 3 m after 5 s, and from then on the other contestant is strictly nearer, before the zombie reaches the owner at 7.14 s (computed).

(c) Under reading A, the immune owner is never a target (this is the reading itself). The owner's zombies chase the nearest non-immune contestant at any distance; where they go depends on the other contestants, never on the owner, so no escort in the sense of (b) exists.

(d) An owner who walks straight on at $v > 1.4$ m/s leaves an escort behind at a rate of at least $v - 1.4$ ([T-0700](../theorems/T-0700.md)(d)), and the zombie may then switch to a nearer contestant.

The theorem is about continuous motion. An implementation that moves zombies and picks targets in discrete steps of $\Delta t$ seconds lets the owner get up to $1.4\,\Delta t$ m ahead of an escort between steps, so a bystander who comes closer than that to the zombie could take it over (`HYPOTHESIS` about the implementation; the step length is unknown).

The same argument applies to any contestant immune to zombies under reading B and K (Shield, Safety bubble, own Sleeping Bag, [M-0454](../rules/zombies.md#m-0454); repellent under [M-0455](../rules/zombies.md#m-0455) reading A): a zombie that reaches a slow immune contestant stays on them while it exists, and drains anyone within 5 m of them who is not immune too. Inside a Safety bubble everyone is immune ([M-0213](../rules/items.md#m-0213)), so only contestants outside the bubble and within 5 m are drained.

*Proof.* (a) While the target is unchanged and moves at speed at most $u = 1.4$, [T-0700](../theorems/T-0700.md)(b) says the distance never increases and stays 0 once it is 0. A switch would need another contestant strictly nearer than distance 0, which is impossible. (b) Summon and horde-bonus zombies appear "where you stand", at distance 0, where the owner is a nearest contestant; apply (a). Drain range is the disk of radius 5 m around the zombie's position, which is the owner's position. For a death zombie that keeps the owner as its target, [T-0701](../theorems/T-0701.md)(c) with $\rho = 0$ gives the time $r/(1.4 - v)$; computed: $10/1.4 = 7.14$ s. In the example, the gaps are $10 - 1.4t$ and $11 - 1.6t$, equal at $t = 5$. (c) Under reading A an immune contestant is not valid, so not a target, and the pursuit law involves only the zombie and its target. (d) [T-0700](../theorems/T-0700.md)(d) bounds the gap from below. $\square$

*Consequence.* Reading B turns the Necromancy sigil into a 5 m drain aura that moves with its owner for as long as each zombie lives ([U-0612](../unknowns.md#u-0612)) and pays the owner in HP and blood tokens ([M-0635](../rules/zombies.md#m-0635), [M-0630](../rules/economy.md#m-0630), [U-0613](../unknowns.md#u-0613)); reading A sends its zombies away to hunt. One observation of a summoned zombie (does it stay with its owner?) decides this, and through it a large part of the sigil's value. (This consequence is `CONDITIONAL` on the same records as the theorem, plus [M-0521](../rules/economy.md#m-0521) for blood tokens in general.)
