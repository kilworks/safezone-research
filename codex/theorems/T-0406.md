**Theorem [T-0406](../theorems/T-0406.md).** (a) The potion decision is not an instance of subfair red-and-black: H2 and H3 fail for every reachable $M$, and H4 fails for every $h \le h_E$. So the bold-play result gives no conclusion here. (H5 fails in any environment that drains HP and H6 whenever the number of potions is finite; these are listed for completeness only.)
(b) What remains true without it: if never drinking means certain failure ($S(h) = 0$) and a success would give a positive chance ($S(h^+) > 0$), then never drinking is not optimal, since drinking now already gives $D(h) \ge S(h^+)/10 > 0 = S(h)$ ([T-0403](../theorems/T-0403.md)). It does not follow that drinking now is optimal: waiting may be better ([T-0404](../theorems/T-0404.md)(a)). Example under [U-0004](../unknowns.md#u-0004) reading A: $M = 100$, each step loses 10 or 20 HP with probability $1/2$ each, $t = 7$ steps left, $h = 70$. Then $S_7(70) = 0$ (seven steps lose at least 70) and $S_7(100) = 29/128$, so drinking now gives $29/1280$, but the optimal holder gets $99/1280$ by waiting: after one step HP is 60 or 50, a success still lands on 100 HP (the cap), and only 6 steps remain instead of 7.
In every case the answer comes from [T-0403](../theorems/T-0403.md) and [T-0404](../theorems/T-0404.md) with the actual $S$.

*Proof.* (a) H2: the outcome of a drink depends on $M$ and $h$ only ([M-0208](../rules/healing.md#m-0208)). H3: $g/\ell = (M/2)/(3M/10) = 5/3$ for every reachable $M$ ([T-0400](../theorems/T-0400.md)). H4: [T-0401](../theorems/T-0401.md)(b). H5 and H6: by definition of the environment and of the inventory. (b) The optimal value is at least the value of drinking now, $D(h) = \frac{1}{10}S(h^+) + \frac{9}{10}S(h^-) \ge S(h^+)/10 > 0$. The example is computed exactly by backward induction. $\square$

The only resemblance is qualitative: in both settings a player who is losing anyway prefers a large risky step to doing nothing. Saying more would be overclaiming.
