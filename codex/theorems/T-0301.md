**Theorem [T-0301](../theorems/T-0301.md).** Let $g$ be a non-decreasing map on integers with $g(y) \ge y$ for every $y \ge 0$. For all integers $x, m \ge 0$:

$$
\min\big(m,\ g(\min(x, m))\big) = \min\big(m,\ g(x)\big).
$$

For the Protection cut $p(y) = \lfloor y/2 \rfloor$ (or $\lceil y/2 \rceil$), the two orders differ exactly when $x > m$, $p(x) > p(m)$ and $m > p(m)$; when they differ, cutting the restored amount gives less.

*Proof.* If $x \le m$ both sides are equal term by term. If $x > m$, the left side is $\min(m, g(m)) = m$ because $g(m) \ge m$; the right side is $\min(m, g(x)) = m$ because $g(x) \ge g(m) \ge m$. For the cut, with $x > m$ the left side is $p(m)$ (as $p(m) \le m$) and the right side is $\min(m, p(x)) \ge p(m)$; they differ iff both $m$ and $p(x)$ exceed $p(m)$. $\square$

*Label:* `DERIVED` (a statement about integer maps, not about the game).

*Consequence* (`CONDITIONAL` on [U-0203](../unknowns.md#u-0203) reading A). With HP capped, the three readings of [U-0503](../unknowns.md#u-0503) give the same HP for a Piety contestant at every HP level: the order of "bonus" and "cap" never matters. They still differ for blood tokens if the token counter uses the nominal heal ([U-0501](../unknowns.md#u-0501)). For Protection the order is a real ambiguity: a gigantic potion drunk at 10 missing HP restores 10 HP if the cut applies to the tier heal ($\min(10, 20)$) and only 5 HP if it applies to the restored amount ($\lfloor 10/2 \rfloor$). This ambiguity has no U-id yet (open question 1 at the end of the chapter).

*Test:* `test_t0301_bonus_commutes_with_the_cap_but_a_cut_does_not` ($m$ in 0..230, $x$ in 0..119, three boosts including Piety's, both cut readings).
