# T-0404

Extracted from [theory/04-probabilistic-items.md](../../theory/04-probabilistic-items.md), where the full context, the proof and the test are.

**Theorem [T-0404](../theorems/T-0404.md).** Consider a pure-loss environment: each step HP changes by $\delta \in \{-c_1, \dots, -c_k, 0\}$ with fixed probabilities, clipped at 0 (0 absorbing), and let $c = \max_i c_i$. A contestant holds one potion and may drink at the start of any step, with $t$ steps left.

(a) If $h > c$ (the next step cannot kill), waiting one step is at least as good as drinking now, under [U-0004](../unknowns.md#u-0004) reading A or C. Hence some optimal policy drinks only at an HP where the next step can kill ($h \le c$).

(b) The set of HP values where drinking now beats never drinking need not be an interval $\{1, \dots, h^*\}$, under reading A or C of [U-0004](../unknowns.md#u-0004).
Example 1 (readings A and C): $M = 100$, one step loses 30 HP with probability $1/2$ (otherwise nothing), $t = 10$. Drinking now beats never drinking exactly for $h \in \{1, \dots, 30\} \cup \{41, \dots, 60\}$, while the optimal holder drinks with 10 steps left exactly for $h \in \{1, \dots, 30\}$: at 41 to 60 HP a drink beats never drinking, but waiting is better still, as (a) predicts since $h > c$.
Example 2 (reading A only): $M = 100$, one step loses 20 HP with probability $1/4$ (otherwise nothing), $t = 10$. Under reading A, drinking now beats never drinking exactly for $h \in \{11, \dots, 20\}$, and this is also the set where the optimal holder drinks with 10 steps left: at 5 HP the contestant waits, at 15 HP they drink. Under reading C the same environment gives $\{1, \dots, 20\}$, an interval, for both sets.

*Proof.* (a) Let $o(x)$ denote the random HP after drinking at $x$. Survival without the potion, $S_t$, is nondecreasing in HP ([T-0403](../theorems/T-0403.md)). For a shift $\delta \le 0$ with $h + \delta \ge 1$: on a success, $\min(M, h + \delta + g) \ge \min(M, h + g) + \delta$; on a failure, reading A gives $\max(h + \delta - \ell, 0) = \max(h - \ell + \delta, 0)$ if $h - \ell \ge 1$, and drinking first leaves the contestant dead otherwise, and reading C gives $\max(h + \delta - \ell, 1) \ge \max(h - \ell, 1) + \delta$. So "move, then drink" leaves HP at least as high as "drink, then move", outcome by outcome, and by monotonicity of $S_{t-1}$:
$$\text{wait} \;\ge\; \sum_\delta p_\delta \sum_o \Pr(o)\, S_{t-1}(o(h + \delta)) \;\ge\; \sum_o \Pr(o) \sum_\delta p_\delta\, S_{t-1}(\mathrm{clip}(o(h) + \delta)) = \text{drink now},$$
where $\mathrm{clip}$ keeps HP in $[0, M]$ and 0 is absorbing.
The first inequality holds because the holder who waits may still drink next step. (b) Example 2 first. Survival without drinking from $x \le 20$ is $(3/4)^{10} = 59049/1048576 \approx 0.0563$; from 51 to 60 HP it is $\Pr(\text{at most 2 hits in 10}) \approx 0.5256 < 10 \times 0.0563$; from 61 to 70 HP it is $\Pr(\text{at most 3 hits}) \approx 0.7759 > 0.5631$. A success from $h \le 10$ lands in 51..60, from $11 \le h \le 20$ in 61..70; by [T-0403](../theorems/T-0403.md)(a) only the second group drinks (reading A); under reading C a failure leaves 1 HP, worth $(3/4)^{10}$, and every $h \le 20$ drinks. Example 1: survival without drinking is $1/1024$, $11/1024$, $56/1024$, $176/1024$ from HP in $1..30$, $31..60$, $61..90$, $91..100$ (at most 0, 1, 2, 3 hits in 10). For $h \le 30$ a success lands in $51..80$, so $D \ge (11/10)/1024 > 1/1024$ whether a failure kills (reading A) or leaves 1 HP (reading C). For $h \ge 31$ a failure leaves $h - 30 \ge 1$ under both readings. For $31 \le h \le 40$: $D = (56/10 + 9/10)/1024 < 11/1024$. For $41 \le h \le 60$: $D = (176/10 + 9/10)/1024 > 11/1024$. For $61 \le h \le 90$, $D \le (176/10 + 9 \cdot 11/10)/1024 < 56/1024$, and for $h \ge 91$, $D \le (176/10 + 9 \cdot 56/10)/1024 < 176/1024$. The holder's set is computed exactly by backward induction. $\square$

*Consequence.* "Drink below some HP threshold" is not the rule. What matters is how many more hits a success buys: damage comes in chunks, and a heal that does not cross a chunk boundary is worth little. The pure-loss hypothesis in (a) matters for the proof: with healing in the environment, a heal after a drink can be capped differently. In 135 holder problems with healing (`test_t0404_heal_sweep_supports_hypothesis`: losses 1 to 35, heals 5 to 40, horizons 5 to 20, reading A) no optimal drink above $c$ was found, which supports (a) for those chains but proves nothing beyond them: `HYPOTHESIS` outside pure-loss environments.

*Label:* (a) `CONDITIONAL` on [U-0004](../unknowns.md#u-0004) reading A or C, [U-0203](../unknowns.md#u-0203) reading A (the proof uses the cap $\min(M, \cdot)$) and Q4.2 (instant drink). (b) `CONDITIONAL` on [U-0203](../unknowns.md#u-0203) reading A and [U-0004](../unknowns.md#u-0004) reading A or C (example 2: reading A only). In both parts the environment is a free parameter, not a rule of the game. *Test:* `test_t0404_waiting_dominates_and_no_threshold` ((a): 16 pure-loss environments, horizons 5, 10, 20, readings A and C, 96 holder problems solved exactly by backward induction; (b): both examples, each under both readings).
