# T-0701

Extracted from [theory/07-zombies-and-pursuit.md](../../theory/07-zombies-and-pursuit.md), where the full context, the proof and the test are.

**Theorem [T-0701](../theorems/T-0701.md).** Take a zombie with drain radius $\rho$ and speed $u$, and a contestant with speed at most $v$ who starts at distance $r_0$.

(a) If $v > u$ and $r_0 \le \rho$, let $\tau^* = (\rho - r_0)/(v - u)$. A contestant who flees in a straight line, directly away from where the zombie stood at time 0, is at distance more than $\rho$ at every time after $\tau^*$, whatever the zombie does. Against a pure-pursuit zombie, every contestant path stays at distance at most $\rho$ during all of $[0, \tau^*]$, and below $\rho$ during $[0, \tau^*)$. So the contestant is in range on $[0, \tau^*]$ under the closed range and on $[0, \tau^*)$ under the open range (Q7-2; empty when $r_0 = \rho$), and under both conventions $\tau^*$ is the exact value of the contest "time spent in range": the contestant cannot do better than straight flight, and the zombie cannot do better than pure pursuit. [T-0013](../theorems/T-0013.md)'s formula is this $\tau^*$ for $\rho = 5$, $u = 1.4$.

(b) If $v \le u$ and $r_0 \le \rho$, a pure-pursuit zombie keeps the contestant in range for as long as it exists and keeps the same target.

(c) If $v < u$ and $r_0 > \rho$, a pure-pursuit zombie is within $\rho$ of the contestant after at most $(r_0 - \rho)/(u - v)$ s, whatever path the contestant takes; a contestant standing still is reached after exactly $(r_0 - \rho)/u$ s. If $v = u$, straight flight keeps the distance at least $r_0$, so the zombie never reaches range.

(d) *Reach radius.* Let the zombie have $\Lambda$ seconds left to live at time 0 ([M-0450](../rules/zombies.md#m-0450)), and say it reaches range within its lifetime if $d(t) \le \rho$ at some $t \le \Lambda$ (closed range, the zombie still existing at the instant $\Lambda$). Against a contestant whose speed is at most $v < u$, the zombie reaches range within its lifetime, whatever the contestant does, if and only if $r_0 \le \rho + (u - v)\Lambda$. Under the open range, or if the zombie is already gone at the instant $\Lambda$, the boundary case (distance exactly $\rho$ exactly at expiry) does not count, and the condition is $r_0 < \rho + (u - v)\Lambda$. A contestant standing still at distance $r_0$ when a horde zombie is released is reached from up to 1685 m if the 20 min count from the release ($\Lambda = 1200$ s, [M-0408](../rules/viewer.md#m-0408), [U-0403](../unknowns.md#u-0403)); a zombie with less time left reaches less far. Over the sweep of [U-0612](../unknowns.md#u-0612) (60 s to 86400 s) the reach radius of a regular zombie against a stationary contestant runs from 89 m to 120965 m.

*Proof.* (a) Straight flight: by [T-0700](../theorems/T-0700.md)(d), $d(t) \ge r_0 + (v - u)t > \rho$ for $t > \tau^*$. Pure pursuit: let $g(t) = r_0 + (v - u)t$. Suppose $d(t_1) > g(t_1)$ for some $t_1$, and let $s$ be the last time before $t_1$ with $d(s) \le g(s)$; then $d(s) = g(s)$ by continuity. On $(s, t_1]$ we have $d > g \ge 0$, so by [T-0700](../theorems/T-0700.md)(b) $d' \le v - u = g'$ there, hence $d(t_1) - g(t_1) \le d(s) - g(s) = 0$, a contradiction. So $d \le g$, and $g(t) \le \rho$ for $t \le \tau^*$, with $g(t) < \rho$ for $t < \tau^*$. Under straight flight against pursuit both move on one line and $d = g$, so $d(\tau^*) = \rho$ exactly: the closed and open conventions differ at that one instant only. (b) [T-0700](../theorems/T-0700.md)(b). (c) The same comparison gives $d(t) \le r_0 - (u - v)t$, which reaches $\rho$ at $(r_0 - \rho)/(u - v)$. A stationary target makes the pursuit a straight walk, so equality holds at $v = 0$. For $v = u$, [T-0700](../theorems/T-0700.md)(d) gives $d(t) \ge r_0$. (d) If $r_0 \le \rho + (u - v)\Lambda$, (c) gives a time at most $\Lambda$ with $d \le \rho$; if $r_0 < \rho + (u - v)\Lambda$, the same comparison gives $d(t) \le r_0 - (u - v)t < \rho$ for $t$ between $(r_0 - \rho)/(u - v)$ and $\Lambda$, a nonempty interval below $\Lambda$, so the open conventions are met too. If $r_0 > \rho + (u - v)\Lambda$, straight flight at speed $v$ keeps $d(t) \ge r_0 - (u - v)t > \rho$ for all $t \le \Lambda$; if $r_0 = \rho + (u - v)\Lambda$, it keeps $d(t) \ge \rho$ for $t \le \Lambda$ with equality only at $t = \Lambda$. $\square$

*Consequence.* "At any distance" ([M-0440](../rules/zombies.md#m-0440)) makes every contestant slower than 1.4 m/s a target that will be reached, unless the zombie expires first or picks someone nearer; (d) says how far "any distance" reaches in practice, once the lifetime is known ([U-0403](../unknowns.md#u-0403), [U-0612](../unknowns.md#u-0612)).

*Label:* `CONDITIONAL` on [M-0440](../rules/zombies.md#m-0440) (speed 1.4 m/s), [U-0424](../unknowns.md#u-0424) (pure pursuit, open plane, same target throughout), [U-0429](../unknowns.md#u-0429) for guard zombies, Q7-2 for which boundary instants count as in range (the value $\tau^*$ is the same under both), and [U-0403](../unknowns.md#u-0403) and [U-0612](../unknowns.md#u-0612) for the time left in (d). A target switch can only end the contact earlier; the straight-flight half of (a) holds for any zombie motion. *Test:* `test_t0701_escape_value` (an exact pursuit on one line in fractions over a grid of $v$ and $r_0$, checking the closed range on $[0, \tau^*]$ and the open range on $[0, \tau^*)$; simulated pursuit against random contestant paths; simulated zombie paths against straight flight; slower contestants for (b) and (c), including $v = u$; the reach radius and its boundary case in exact fractions).
